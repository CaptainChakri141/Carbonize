Understanding the Breakdown

Every activity leaves a trace. Here is what constitutes each segment of your carbon footprint:

Transportation: Includes personal vehicle fuel combustion, diesel engine operations, public transit hours, and commercial aviation trips.
Home Utility & Energy: Includes electricity consumption from your utility provider (offset by solar share) and natural gas, heating oil, or boiler utility emissions.
Diet & Food Sourcing: Driven by dietary preferences (meat vs. plant-based) and balanced by sustainable, local-sourcing habits.
Shopping & Consumption: Driven by consumer behavior (new garments, electronics) and reduced by recycling and repair frequency.

Transportation

The True Cost of Driving

Passenger vehicles are a major driver of global warming. On average, a standard gasoline car emits 400 grams of CO2 for every mile driven. Switching to carpooling or biking just twice a week can slash transit emissions by 20%.

Read EPA Guide
Food Sourcing

Plates and the Planet

Dietary emissions vary wildly. Red meat (beef and lamb) production yields up to 30x more carbon than plant-based proteins due to land clearance, logistics, and methane. Going vegetarian or vegan is the single most effective way to lower personal carbon footprint.

Read WRI Insights
Home Power

Smart Grid Solutions

Where your electricity comes from matters. Utility grids heavily reliant on coal/natural gas have a high emission coefficient. Purchasing utility green power certificates or using LED bulbs (which run 85% cooler and require less watts) mitigates this cost.
